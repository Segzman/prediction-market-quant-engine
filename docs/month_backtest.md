## Conclusion

No model beats the contract-price baseline on Brier with a 95% CI below zero over the month. The simulated trades of A, B, C, P have a mean realized PnL per trade whose 95% CI lies above zero after fees and slippage in this simplified sim. Treat the result as a first month-long measurement, not a live edge claim: the trade sim omits exits, hedges and book-depth gating, and the feature set is approximated.

## Limitations

- Window open and decision prices come from Binance BTCUSDT 1-second klines. Polymarket resolves on Chainlink BTC/USD (`resolutionSource`), so the open and final prices can differ slightly from the settlement reference.
- Contract prices come from the CLOB prices-history endpoint at its finest fidelity (about one point per minute with gaps). A quote must be no older than 120 s; otherwise the state is dropped and counted above.
- Features are approximations of the bot's live features. The contract quote is the mid of the Up and Down tokens, normalized; market conviction is proxied by the strongest-side price; book depth, queue imbalance, microprice and one-sidedness are set to neutral because the history has no book data. Model C is calibrated on all prior decision states, not only on the bot's traded resolutions.
- The trading sim is a simplified rule set: fixed stake, no exits, no hedges, no late-conviction regime, no fill or queue model beyond a flat slippage charge.
- The sim's positive PnL is most likely a data artifact, not an edge: contract quotes are up to 120 s old while BTC prices are per second, so a model can react to BTC moves the stale quote has not priced yet. The same models lose to the market on Brier, which is the cleaner test. Live trading would face fresh quotes and real fills.
- Decision states inside one window are correlated. Brier and hit-rate CIs use Wilson intervals on states, while the paired Brier differences and trade PnL CIs resample whole windows.
